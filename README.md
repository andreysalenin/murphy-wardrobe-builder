# Murphy Wardrobe Builder

**Live page: https://andreysalenin.github.io/murphy-wardrobe-builder/**

A one-page tool for designing a built-in plywood wardrobe around a Create-A-Bed vertical queen Murphy bed: a fold-down bed in the middle, a tower of drawers and hanging space either side, and lift-up flaps over a storage shelf above the bed. Change a size and the 3D model, the bed's swing clearances, the cut list and the sheet nesting update straight away. Then save:

- **Drawings**: an A3 PDF set of 20 sheets: overview, elevations and plans, sections with the bed's swing, sections and clear openings (also live on the page, below), the bed frame with the kit's numbers, hardware positions, clearances, cut list and hardware, cutting plans, a build sequence (a day-by-day timeline and 19 steps, each with a picture and its fixings), assembly details, a fixings schedule (screws, dowels, drill sizes, glue, wall fixings, parts by step) and a design review. Everything is vector, the 3D views included: they are drawn as shaded faces in exact depth order, so they stay sharp at any zoom and print cleanly.
- **Fixings, kept few**: six kinds of screw plus the wall screws, and one 8 mm dowel. Every edge joint is an 8g x 50, every face-to-face joint an 8g x 30, the drawer fronts 8g x 25 pan heads, every 6 and 12 mm part a 6g x 25, all the hinge, stay, runner and rail hardware a 3.5 x 15, and the runners on the 12 mm drawer sides a 3.5 x 10. Three driver bits (T15, T20, T30). No Domino or biscuit joiner and no nailer: the divider is dowelled like the upper sides, the header battens are glued and clamped, and the kick board is bonded with nothing in its face.
- **Bed face with nothing through it**: the face panel is glued and dowelled to the rails from behind; the struts and the kit's 1/4 in mattress support (part J, with 90 vent holes of 25 mm between the struts) are screwed on from behind. Options: cut the support wide + narrow so its seam is off the face joint, and a 6 mm reveal down the middle of the face in line with the flap joint above.
- **Headboard storage unit**: the backrest leans back at an adjustable angle over a storage unit with a 6 mm back. A services zone (100 mm by default) at each tower holds a flush Australian double power point, the lamp cables and the hole into the tower, behind a removable panel; then three compartments (a pillow, the doona, a pillow) on four supports (18 mm under the joints between doors, 12 at the ends), behind three doors. The panels have 6 mm reveals centred on the supports, overlapping the middle ones by 6 and the end ones by 3. Each door stands on tilt-out hinges with a stop and leans back closed under its own weight; a push latch under the shelf pops it 5 mm proud to pull. Doors, panels and shelf meet in a mitre at (90 + lean) / 2 with a 2 mm gap. The doors tilt open in the 3D view. A check warns when the lean leaves less than the clear depth the power points need under the shelf (110 mm by default) and gives the most lean that keeps it.
- **Wall gap and packers**: the carcass stands 6 mm off the back wall; packers stacked from 6 and 12 mm offcuts fill the gap behind the wall rails and the headboard cleat at every stud.
- **Safety lock options**: the twin pin behind the flaps, two drop pins, or slim side bolts (a spring index plunger in each hanging space, 18 proud of the tower side, its pin into the bed's side rail), each drawn on sheet 6.
- **Flap hinges**: unsprung Blum CLIP top (70T3550), so the push latches can open the flaps; the doors keep BLUMOTION.
- **Cable holes for power and light**: one 20 mm route a side, from the space behind the headboard backrest (where a wall outlet, the bedside power units and a 24 V supply live) up through the tower to the bed bay and the box over the bed. They are cut in the model (STEP and viewer), drawn on sheets 6 and 8 with positions per part, and the tests check each one clears every screw and the hardware. The bedside shelf is screwed only, so it lifts off as a hatch.
- **Assembly details**: a half page for every step that joins plywood. Each operation is an exploded view at one scale, with the part going on pulled back along its joint, each screw floating off its head side, and a dashed line from its tip through the clearance hole to where it lands. Dowels float in the gap. Tags name the fixings (A is an 8g x 50, DW an 8 x 30 dowel, and so on), and numbered, coloured circles give the driving order where it matters: the front screw of a carcass joint first, a back's four corners first, the steel flat from the middle out. Close-ups at 1:5 or larger show a typical joint. Dimensions are drawn on the views themselves: a chain along each line of holes from the edge of the part the fixing goes through, centre to centre, with equal gaps written once (50 · 6 × 77.5 · 50), and a chain across the face that places each line from the edges. Screws are spaced on whole-millimetre pitches so the chains stay short, and lines that repeat are dimensioned once. The first half page draws every fixing full size as a key. The hole positions come from one joinery model, which the tests check for each fixing: inside the parts it joins, clear of their faces, room to drive it, and no clashes with another fixing.
- **Section view**: a Section button beside 3D, Front, Side and Top draws sheet 4 live as you change sizes, zoomable and pannable: a front section with the fronts, bed and drawers out (A-A), a plan (B-B) above it, and side sections through a tower (C-C) and a headboard compartment (D-D) to its right, in third-angle projection at one scale. Each space carries its clear width and height, the views carry the dimensions that make them up, and a table lists every clear opening (wide, high, deep) with the hinge arms and runners noted.
- **3D model**: a STEP (AP214) assembly for CAD, one solid per part, with the hardware and mattress if you want them, and the bed closed or at the slider's angle.
- **Cut list**: a CSV of every part, with the sheet each piece is nested on.

Everything runs in the browser and nothing is uploaded. The page is a single self-contained file (`index.html`), so it also works offline.

## What you can change

- **Cavity**: width, height and depth wall to wall (2522 x 2564 x 600 by default), widths at the front and back for walls that are out of square, packing or planing at each of the four corners, studs standing proud of the side walls (none by default: the nibs are coming out), the gap at the back, and how far the fronts sit back from the room wall.
- **Head of the opening and cornices**: the 90x42 left across the top of the mouth, with the plasterboard on it cut under its cornices, comes 55 down from the ceiling by default; the carcass slides in 6 under it (a shadow line the size of the reveals), and a header filler fills any bigger gap. The 90x42 stops 7 short of the plasterboard's cut edges on either face; a 6 mm plywood soffit glued up under it (in the cut list, two halves) closes that to 1, so the shadow line shows one clean underside. Cornices are 55 each way, a 10 mm lip square off the wall, a 45 radius cove and a 10 mm lip square off the ceiling; each run (back wall, side walls, both faces of the head) can be taken off. They show in the 3D view and in the sections, and the checks say what runs into the head or a cornice, by how much, and how far to bring it down.
- **The room**: 3116 along the wardrobe wall and 3168 away from it, its right wall in line with the opening's right side, its ceiling and cornices, the door in its left wall with its swing, and the window in its right wall (1800 x 1000, sill 1200, 675 from the wardrobe wall, in the 185 external wall: a 140 sill board, a black aluminium frame, two 900 x 1000 panes, the right one an awning open 15 degrees). By the far wall, two Castlery Emmerson standing desks (1.4 m, walnut, the top's front edge set back 50 in a shallow arc, 40 corners) sit in its corners 5 off the walls with an IKEA ALEX (grey-turquoise by default, any colour) between them; the desks rise just enough to clear it, and the checks say how far the bed's foot comes from them. The furniture models come from the studies in `study/furniture` (dimensions, sources and what is estimated). Tick Room beside the 3D view to see it; near walls drop away so you look in. The checks flag the door opening back across the left tower.
- **Skins**: an optional second layer of plywood on each tower's outer side, any thickness ("12 + 18", "18 + 18"), from the back of the carcass to the face of the fronts so its edge shows as a plywood stripe beside the doors. It takes the scribe to the wall and the fronts stop a reveal short of it. By default it starts a reveal above the kick board, level with the drawer fronts' bottom edge, with the kick running under it wall to wall as one band, and stops at the carcass top: 2433 tall, so it comes out of one 2440 sheet. It can also start on the floor (the kick between the skins) or stop at the ceiling. The page warns when a skin is longer than your sheets of that thickness and says how to make it fit. The kick board and header come in two halves butted on the centre line whenever one sheet is too short for them.
- **Mattress and bed**: mattress size, the gaps round it (which set the frame size), the 12 mm gap between the closed bed and the shelf above it, and where the bed face panel and frame start off the floor.
- **Side towers**: number and heights of the drawers, drawer runners (drawn as their three nested members), the hanging space, an extra shelf in the top compartment, and whether the doors hinge on the wall side or the bed side.
- **Bought-in hardware**: drawer runners are Hettich KA 5632 (full extension, 45 kg, 46 x 12.7, 250 to 700 long) and hinges are Blum CLIP top BLUMOTION 110° full overlay (71B3550) on CLIP cruciform plates, both sold in Australia. The page picks the runner length that fits, and the hinge plate (0 or 3 mm) and boring distance for each overlay from Blum's table. It models both to the makers' dimensions and puts the runner holes and hinge positions on the drawings.
- **Bed gap**: the bed-cabinet sides stand 11 mm (7/16 in) off the bed frame's side rails: the kit's plastic pivot spacer and male plate fill that gap, so the kit's cabinet plates screw straight on. The gas springs do not run in it. Both ball studs point in (the lower one through a tab on its plate that reaches past the rail's top edge), so each 22 mm spring runs along its rail's top edge, behind the rail when the bed is closed, about 5 mm from the side. What sits in the gap is the plates, the bed stops and the lower stud's nut. A wider gap (your original drawing had 30 mm) is still an option, with small pads under the cabinet plates to bring them back to the kit's spacing; at 11 mm each tower is 19 mm wider inside. The bed's face panel overhangs its side rails by 5 mm (the kit's plan does 1/4 in), and the tower doors and drawer fronts run in over the bed-cabinet sides' front edges, so the reveal beside the bed is 6 mm like every other front, in one line from the kick board to the header with the flaps above the bed.
- **Clearances**: every clearance the build depends on, worked out from the design and listed on the page and on sheet 7. That covers the gap beside the bed and what is in it, the gas springs through the bed's swing, the bed against everything around it, the fronts and fittings, and fitting the cabinet in the room. Sheet 7 has true sections through the gap at 1:1 (at the lower ball stud, the pivot and the top of a spring), the springs through the swing, and the kit sizes the numbers rest on. The booklet gives the hardware's positions, not its sizes, so the plate and nut, ball stud, spring, stop domes and legs are settings: measure yours and the clearances follow. The bed stops go where the closed bed stands plumb, so its face is flush with the flaps.
- **Kick board and header**: each is one plank right across. The kick board is as tall as clears the bed's face panel as it swings down, so the drawers and tower plinths drop to sit on it and the hanging space gets the difference. The planks run as long as your longest sheet allows.
- **Fronts and plywood**: reveals, the carcass top below the ceiling, and the thickness used for carcass, drawers, backs, deck, plinths, pads and the headboard. The kick boards and header filler are 12 by default (the same birch as the 18); the plinth fronts come forward to meet the thinner kick. The tower tops and the top over the bed are 18, and the adjustable shelves are 18 too, level with the shelf over the bed so one line runs across with the doors open; they go in last, on their pins. The headboard ledge's two big panels, its front and the well floor, are 12 with three 12 mm ribs behind the front, floor to the cleats (the sill and the wall cleat stay 18: they carry the hinges and the screws into the studs); that keeps the 18 mm parts on eight sheets. When the parts do not fit the sheets you have, the cutting plans lay what is left over on the sheets to buy, marked as such.
- **Create-A-Bed hardware**: pivot, gas-spring and bed-stop positions, frame rails, legs and deck struts, each with the page of the Deluxe Queen Vertical plan it comes from, so they are easy to revise against your kit, plus the hardware sizes to measure.
- **Around the bed**: a headboard ledge behind the pillows, and a backrest that leans back over the bed's swing with a bedside shelf on top.
- **Shelf over the bed**: a box of the shelf, two upper bed-cabinet sides (so the doubled 2 x 18 mm sides run the full height, broken only where the shelf passes through), the top and a 6 mm back that ties it together, built on the floor and slid in. The shelf is flat 18 mm, carried by the centre divider, with a 20 x 10 steel flat hidden in its front edge (2 mm proud underneath, behind the flaps), or a lip if you prefer one. The safety lock is a twin pin: two 10 mm pins on one bar either side of the divider, going into the bed's foot rail, lifted with one pull through either flap. An optional tab through the joint between the flaps lets you lift it without opening a flap. Two separate pins mid-bay, or no lock, are also options.
- **Sheets and cutting**: the sheets you have in each thickness, kerf, edge trim and grain direction. Parts are nested onto them with straight, track-saw cuts.
- **3D view**: click any door, drawer or flap to open or close just that one (click the bed to swing it down or back up). "Open fronts" opens or shuts them all, and shows a grey dash when only some are open. Also fronts on or off, a cut through the bed, mattress, hardware, colour by sheet thickness, and a slider (or play button) that swings the bed down with the gas springs, flap stays and lock moving.

The page checks the design as you go: the bed's swing against the floor, the shelf and flaps, the headboard and the kick board; the gas springs and the kit's plates, nuts and stops in the gap beside the bed; the shelf's sag; the legs against the kit's pivot height; and whether the parts fit on your sheets. Settings are remembered in the browser.

## Building

`index.html` is generated from the sources:

```
npm install
npm run build      # writes index.html
```

- `src/page.html`: markup and styles
- `src/core.js`: parameters, geometry, swing clearances, checks, cut list, nesting, STEP writer (also runs in Node)
- `src/viewer.js`: three.js viewer and the renders used in the drawings
- `src/drawings.js`: the A3 drawing set (jsPDF)
- `src/app.js`: page wiring

three.js r128 and jsPDF 2.5.1 (both MIT licensed) are inlined by `build.js`.

## Tests

- `npm run check`: builds three designs in Node and checks cut sizes against the solids, part clashes, the nesting and the design checks, then that the plywood fits the sheets on hand, every screw and dowel in the joinery model, then samples every piece of hardware against every other part with the bed closed, half open and down (balls in their sockets, the pivot bar in its bush and the like are listed as intended).
- `node test/export.js [params.json]`: opens `index.html` in headless Chromium and saves the PDF, STEP, CSV and two screenshots to `out/` (set `CHROMIUM_PATH` to a Chromium or headless-shell binary).
- `python3 test/check_step.py out/default.step`: loads the STEP file with OpenCASCADE (needs Python with cadquery-ocp) and reports solids, validity, volume and bounds.

## The shelf over the bed

`study/` holds the plate model and scripts behind the shelf: why a lip isn't needed once the divider is glued and screwed, why the steel flat is there, which boxes fit in two or three bays, and where the lock can go. See `study/README.md`.

## Please note

The drawings are a starting point for building, not certified engineering. Check every Create-A-Bed position against the instructions that come with your kit, anchor the cabinet to the studs as the kit asks, and measure your cavity, ceiling and floor before cutting.
